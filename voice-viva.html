What the examiner will and will not do

A viva is only useful if the examiner behaves like one.

It will
  • Present a concise clinical scenario
  • Ask one question at a time
  • Let you answer fully before responding
  • Probe incomplete answers
  • Stop cleanly at the end of the station
  • Give structured feedback afterwards
  • Name safety-critical omissions
  • Ask one near-transfer question
It will not
  • Interrupt you mid-answer
  • Coach you during the station
  • Hint before you commit
  • Accept a vague answer as complete
  • Reassure you instead of examining you
  • Certify your competence

If the examiner does any of the six on the right, that is a defect worth reporting. Use the feedback form and choose “Question or station quality”.

Before you start

Voice mode is a feature of ChatGPT, not of OrthoSG. Press the voice control, say your instruction, and the station begins. You can switch back to typing at any point.

Speech recognition is imperfect, particularly with drug names and eponyms. If a word is misheard, correct it and carry on — the examiner will not penalise a transcription error, but it is worth reporting if it happens repeatedly.

Voice-derived scores are formative only. They are not evidence of clinical competence.
